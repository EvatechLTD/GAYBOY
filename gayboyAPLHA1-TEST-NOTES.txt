Four independent reviews covered sequencing/live launch, audio ownership,
UI/navigation/VRAM safety, and memory/stability. Emulator verification is on
DMG timing. This is a hardware-test alpha, not a claim of hardware certification.

Fixes integrated:
- DSP table relocation eliminates a confirmed stack/table collision and gives
  the stack a 512-byte region. Stack stress retains IRQ reserve.
- Scheduler catches up overdue steps in one call. Playhead drawing runs in
  the UI loop, reducing accumulated timing drift during held scrolling.
- Native pattern tone recalls retain timer phase and PCM ownership.
- Faster bit serialization and ROM schema/scatter preserve old saved tones.
- MIDI raw pitch CCs cannot retune PULSEKICK while it owns Pulse 1.
- Runtime font refresh uses safe VRAM uploads under PCM interrupts.
- Drum velocity lane refreshes when the selected row changes.
- PK resumes a still-gated P1 note; same-pattern launches restart at the
  quantized downbeat without releasing every voice.

Measured remaining limitations:
- Dense 512-event live Mixer pattern preparation is synchronous. Maximum
  observed button acknowledgement: 1.44 s at 180 BPM, 1.93 s at 220 BPM,
  2.80 s at 280 BPM. Audio continues during preparation.
- Those dense live-launch cases show approximately 33 ms peak-to-peak note
  jitter and do not meet the existing 12 ms live timing target. Audio underrun
  counters remained zero; that does not prove perfect analog audio delivery.
- At 220 BPM, held native scrolling measured 10.94 ms onset jitter with stable
  note counts and no accumulating backlog. Typical native handoffs are about
  11?12 ms. No tempo cap was added; 300 BPM remains an overload test.
- The separate PCM timing suite reports failures of first-step/delivery
  targets at 300 BPM. Its detailed report is retained; no thresholds were
  relaxed to mark it passing.

Hardware test priorities:
1. Hear sustained P1 notes resume after PK, including kicks mid-note.
2. Relaunch the current pad with BEAT/BAR quantization; check downbeat restart.
3. Switch saved patterns at your usual tempo while playing and navigating.
4. Hold scroll/edit controls while drums and pulse notes play.
5. Exercise experimental synthesis/FX and save/load; check for crashes.
6. Save, power off, and verify battery recall with your cartridge.
