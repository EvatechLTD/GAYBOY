CORRECT THE PREVIOUS AGENT'S DIAGNOSIS

I over-attributed the problem to pulse note handling. Pulse event lookup,
pitch/velocity calculation and held-note bookkeeping do delay audible attacks,
but that does NOT establish why external LSDj sync fixes the whole sequencer.
The pulse-only IRQ optimization below is partial work, not proof of root cause.
Its independent attack path could keep pulses on time while other channels
remain late, accentuating relative separation. Measure the WHOLE STEP.

Confirmed from current source:
  Internal clock: elapsed musical time comes from local timer/audio accounting.
  LSDj slave: local musical-time accumulation is bypassed; incoming serial
  clock pulses are converted into sequencer time. Actual note dispatch is still
  predominantly shared foreground code in both cases.
This makes internal elapsed-time accounting, wake-up/catch-up scheduling,
interrupt latency and preservation of time across launches prime suspects.
These are hypotheses to test, not established causes.

  serial_isr queues incoming bytes; service/mixfill parses them.
  mixfill runs seq_frame only when RX drained, !seqservicing and

  Native nonPCM: n=64-(TMA>>2), seq_irq_tick(n), seqticks+=n.
  Slave does not advance musical time from that local timer path.

src/drum.c drum_timer (~line369): naked BANKED, serial-only preemption;
  if playing and NOT sync slave, seqfraction adds183/64; integer contribution

  interrupted/masked sections and any clock loss, not just average audio rate.
  seq_next_deadline (~line847) determines foreground scheduler wake-up.

src/sync.c sync_advance (~line90), receive, sync_receive:
  Slave pending pulses converted with quotient/remainder into seqticks.
  LSDj BPM estimate uses a full quarter note of sys_time, not each pulse.
  sync_receive immediately adds converted pulse time; mixfill later dispatches.

src/sequencer.c seq_frame (~line135):
  Atomic snapshot seqticks then clears it; processes time slices <=128 units.
  Advances gates and clock; at step boundary subtracts threshold, updates swing.
  Publishes remaining local ticks before mixer_boundary callback, subtracts
  them back if no handoff occurred. New ticks during callback remain global.
  All track positions advance, then seq_fire(6) drums FIRST, followed by
  melodic tracks in order. These can do lookup/banked calls/seq_audio/mixfill.
  seqservicing prevents recursive sequencer dispatch, but audio/serial continue.
  Finally seq_next_deadline, seq_irq_prepare, clears seqservicing.
  seq_pattern_start (~line110) resets positions, runs IRQ pulse launch batch,
  recalculates gates if needed, clock reset, sync marker, drums, then melodies.

src/sparse_store.c sparse_restore/activate/request/service:
  Native live handoff restores metadata/tones, releases outgoing voices,
